# 30 Prompts for Midterm Rental Operators

Copy, paste, edit the bracketed parts. These assume you have a `CLAUDE.md` from Lesson 1, so they will use your market, your units, and your rates without being told.

Gift from Lesson 1 of the MTR Operator Toolkit.

---

## Finding demand (1 to 6)

1. Which five employers in my market are most likely to need 30 to 90 day housing this year, and what is the signal for each?
2. Find construction or infrastructure projects in my market running longer than six months. Name the contractor and where I can verify it.
3. What are the residency and fellowship start dates for the hospital systems in my market, and how far ahead do they arrange housing?
4. Which relocation management companies place people into my market? What is the usual way an operator gets onto their vendor list?
5. Read this job posting and tell me whether the employer is likely to be paying for housing: [paste posting]
6. What time of year does demand peak in my market for each of my target client types, and what should I be doing sixty days before each peak?

## Outreach (7 to 12)

7. Write a first-touch email to [role] at [company]. Under 120 words, first sentence about their situation, one ask for fifteen minutes.
8. Rewrite this so the first sentence is about them instead of me: [paste your email]
9. This email got no reply after two weeks. Tell me honestly why, then rewrite it: [paste]
10. Give me a 7-touch follow-up over 14 days for [company]. Every touch delivers something they would keep. None of them says "just checking in".
11. Write a LinkedIn connection note to a corporate mobility manager at [company]. Under 300 characters.
12. Write the voicemail script for a restoration company owner I have never spoken to. Thirty seconds.

## Handling the conversation (13 to 18)

13. Give me five discovery questions for a first call with a relocation coordinator that reveal volume, timing, and who actually approves.
14. They said my rate is higher than what they usually pay. Give me three responses that hold the rate and one that trades scope instead of price.
15. Write my one-line answer to "so what do you do?" for a chamber event. No jargon.
16. They asked how many units I have and I have two. Write the honest answer that still sounds like a vendor.
17. What questions should I be asking to find out if this company has a real budget or is just shopping?
18. Turn this call into a follow-up email with clear next steps: [paste your notes]

## Pricing and deals (19 to 22)

19. My all-in monthly cost on this unit is [X]. Work out my pricing floor for a 30-day stay at 85% collection, and show the maths.
20. Build me three service tiers for the same unit: standard, priority, white-glove. What actually differs, and what does each add to the price?
21. Here is a deal I am considering: [details]. What is my break-even, what would have to be true for it to work, and what is the risk nobody mentions?
22. Compare arbitrage against co-hosting for this specific unit, with my numbers, and tell me which fits my situation.

## Guests and operations (23 to 27)

23. Write my check-in message for a 60-day corporate guest. Warm, brief, and it sets expectations without a wall of rules.
24. A guest reported [issue] on day 3. Write my reply. Fix first, no defensiveness.
25. It is day 45 of a 60-day stay. Write the renewal conversation opener.
26. Write my ask for an honest review at checkout. Never request a rating and never offer anything in exchange.
27. Turn this into a checklist my VA can follow without asking me anything: [describe your process]

## The business (28 to 30)

28. Look at my numbers and tell me which unit is actually my worst performer once my time is counted: [paste]
29. What should I stop doing this quarter? Be specific and tell me what to do with the time.
30. If I wanted one corporate account that replaced ten Airbnb bookings, what would the next 30 days need to look like?
