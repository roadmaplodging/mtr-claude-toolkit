# 5 Agent Workflows for MTR Operators

An agent workflow is several Claudes working at once instead of one working in sequence. Paste one of these and it runs the whole thing.

Gift from Lesson 3 of the MTR Operator Toolkit.

---

## 1. Market entry scan

> Run three agents in parallel on [city, state]. Agent 1: employers expanding, relocating, or hiring in waves, plus universities. Agent 2: hospital systems, residency intakes, travel nurse demand, and construction projects over six months. Agent 3: existing furnished and corporate housing operators, their rough 30 to 90 day pricing, and the gaps. Every row needs a named source and a confidence label of Verified, Likely, or Unconfirmed. Then score the market out of 10 and give me a go or no-go, and tell me the one row to verify first.

Use before signing anything in a new market.

## 2. Target list build

> Build me a list of 30 companies in [city] that plausibly need 30 to 90 day housing. Run agents across four categories at once: healthcare, construction and engineering, corporate relocation, and insurance restoration. For each company give me the name, why they qualify, the role most likely to own housing decisions, and where I can verify. Rank by how reachable they are for a small operator, not by how big they are.

Use at the start of any outreach push. Feed the winners to `/mtr:outreach`.

## 3. Competitor teardown

> Research the furnished and corporate housing operators serving [city]. For each: how they position, roughly what they charge for 30 to 90 days, what client types they name, and what compliance or vendor language they use. Then tell me the three things they all say, and the one thing none of them says that I could own.

The last question is the one that matters. What they all say is the category. What none of them says is the position.

## 4. Weekly operator brief

> Every Monday, run this. Search for news in [city] from the last seven days on: employer expansions or layoffs, hospital or health system announcements, construction starts, and fires or floods that would create displacement. Give me a short brief with a named source per item, and for each one, a single sentence on what an MTR operator should do about it this week.

This is the whole "Market Monday" habit, automated. Post the best signal in the community.

## 5. Deal pressure test

> Here is a deal I am considering: [paste details, rent, furnishing cost, projected rate, market]. Run three agents against it. Agent 1: verify the demand assumptions with live sources. Agent 2: check the competitive rate for that unit type in that submarket. Agent 3: find the regulatory position on 30+ day stays in that jurisdiction. Then tell me the break-even, the three assumptions the deal depends on, and what would have to be true for me to walk away.

Run this before every lease. The regulatory agent alone justifies the exercise.

---

**Agent research is a scout report, not a decision.** Verify the rows you are about to spend money on. And whether a 30-day stay creates a tenancy where you operate is a question for local counsel, never for a language model.
