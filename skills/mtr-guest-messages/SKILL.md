# Guest messages for midterm stays

This is the message cadence we run across our own furnished portfolio, generalized. The timing is the doctrine; the words adapt to the operator.

## Adapt to this operator first

Read `CLAUDE.md` (or `mtr-profile.md`) before drafting anything. Their market, their target client, and their voice shape every message. If their profile says no units live yet, say so plainly and offer to build the sequence as a template for their first guest instead — writing it before it is needed is a fine use of a quiet week. Never reference amenities, policies, or services the profile does not support.

## The cadence, and why it is shaped this way

A midterm guest is not a hotel guest and not a tenant. Over-messaging reads as noise; silence reads as absence. The cadence is a handful of well-timed touches with deliberate quiet in between:

| When | Message | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| Booking confirmed | Confirmation + what happens next | Kills the "did that go through?" anxiety |
| A few days before arrival | Practical arrival details | Everything they need, nothing they don't |
| Check-in day | Short welcome, how to reach you | Sets the response expectation once |
| **Day 3** | **Settle-in check** | **The highest-value message in the stay — see below** |
| Mid-stay (around the halfway mark) | Brief checkpoint | Catches slow-building issues; signals presence without hovering |
| **14 days before checkout** | **The renewal conversation** | A renewal is a vacancy that never happened |
| Checkout | Thanks + departure practicalities | Clean close |
| After checkout | One honest review ask, one gentle nudge later if silent, then stop | Reviews compound; pestering destroys them |

**Silence in between is part of the design.** If a touch has no purpose, do not send it.

## The day-3 message

A guest who says the shower runs cold on day 3 gives you most of the stay to fix it. The same guest, silent until checkout, gives you a bad review and no recourse. So day 3 asks one open question — "how is the place treating you, anything not working the way it should?" — and nothing else. No upsell, no rules reminder. Make it easy to report a problem and easy to say all is well.

## The renewal conversation

For a 30 to 90 day operator, the renewal is the business. Fourteen days before checkout, ask about their plans before they have made other ones: whether the assignment or project is extending, and that you would be glad to keep them if it is. Two weeks out is early enough to matter and late enough that they usually know. If they are leaving, the same message converts to an early heads-up on your calendar — either answer is worth having.

## The review ask

Ask once, at or just after checkout, for an **honest review**. One nudge about a week later if they have not responded, then stop forever.

Rules that do not bend, because they carry account-level risk on every platform:

- Never ask for a rating, a star count, or a "positive" review. Ask for an honest one.
- Never offer anything in exchange for a review, and never imply the review affects anything.
- Never argue with or work around a bad review in guest messages. That conversation is the operator's, offline.

## Voice

Warm, brief, and specific. No exclamation walls, no corporate boilerplate, no wall of house rules disguised as a welcome. Every message should read like a competent human who expects things to go well and is easy to reach if they don't.

## Rules that do not bend

- **The operator sends. You draft.** Never imply a message was or will be sent automatically.
- Every factual claim (wifi speed, parking, distances, policies) traces to the operator's profile. Missing fact: ask or leave it out.
- Nothing in a guest message ever discusses pricing changes mid-stay, and nothing ever pressures. A pressured guest is a bad review waiting to be written.
